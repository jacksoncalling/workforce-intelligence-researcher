## Signal: Interview process length has doubled for mid-level roles in NRW

**Date:** 2026-05-31
**Region:** NRW
**Industry:** Tech, Consulting, Finance
**Type:** hiring-behavior
**Lens:** Digitalization × Decent Work
**Confidence:** Medium (3 coaches, 5 coachees — consistent pattern across sectors)

### Observation

Coachees applying for mid-level positions (3–7 yrs experience) report 5–6 rounds as the new norm — screening call, HR interview, hiring manager, team fit, case study, final leadership round. Six months ago 2–3 rounds were standard for the same seniority level. Two coachees dropped out after round 4 due to time pressure. Others report feeling unable to withdraw despite the burden because of unemployment pressure.

### Context

This sits at a sharp intersection: digitalization (automated screening adds rounds without reducing total time) × decent work (unemployed candidates cannot exercise the same leverage as employed candidates in saying no to excessive processes). The asymmetry is the signal — employed candidates drop out, unemployed candidates comply. Employers may be inadvertently filtering for desperation.

### Coaching Implication

Reframe expectation-setting early in coaching. A long process is not a red flag — it is the new normal. Prepare coachees mentally for 6–8 week timelines and coach them on energy management across rounds. Importantly: name the power asymmetry with the coachee. They are allowed to ask the recruiter how many rounds remain. That question is not disqualifying — it is professional.
