## Signal: AI tool proficiency appearing in non-tech job descriptions

**Date:** 2026-05-10
**Region:** National (observed across NRW and Bayern)
**Industry:** Administration, Marketing, HR
**Type:** skills-demand
**Lens:** Digitalization
**Confidence:** Low-Medium (2 coaches, 4 coachees — emerging pattern)

### Observation

Coachees applying for administrative and marketing roles report seeing "experience with AI tools" or "ChatGPT/Copilot proficiency" listed in job requirements — positions where this was absent 6 months ago. In interviews, the depth of "AI proficiency" expected varies wildly: some employers want prompt engineering skills, others just want confirmation the candidate has used ChatGPT at all. Two coachees report that demonstrating AI awareness in the interview (even basic) shifted the conversation positively.

### Context

This is new. Six months ago, AI requirements appeared only in tech-adjacent roles. The spread into administration and marketing suggests a normalization phase. However, the inconsistency in what "AI proficiency" means creates uncertainty for coachees — should they invest in learning specific tools, or is general awareness sufficient?

### Coaching Implication

Coaches should add a brief AI literacy check to early sessions: does the coachee know what these tools are? Have they tried them? For coachees in administration/marketing, a 30-minute introduction to ChatGPT or Copilot may become as standard as the resume review. But do NOT position this as "you must learn AI" — position it as "here is how to talk about AI in interviews even if your daily use is minimal." The interview signal matters more than the actual skill right now.
